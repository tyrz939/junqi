# Jane — Story

**Status: settled, 26 September 2026.** This is the account of what is true in Castle and built in `data/`: the spine from the platform to the three endings, the one revelation each act gives, and how the town hears what she does. It was drafted from the 2020 material (`DESIGN-2020.md`), the Trello boards (`OLD-NOTES.md`) and the build; §7 records the five choices John decided, and everything below follows from them. Where a row or a test holds a claim, it is named.

The story is not the point of Jane. The place is. This document exists so the place is **consistent**: so every sign, room, person and enemy can be checked against one account of what is true in Castle. `VOICE.md` says how it is written; `WORLD.md` how the county lives; `QUEST-TREE.md` §5 what she is asked; `EXPERIENCE.md` what she meets, in order.

## 1. The premise

Castle is a county that has come loose from the rest of the world.

- One train a week, on Sunday: in at five, out at two minutes past. Twelve hours from the city. No signal after the tunnel. A letter that looks a hundred years old arrives on the right birthday. **[2020]**
- In the tunnel Jane sees coloured things floating and hears them singing "about Day and night". **[2020]** The butterflies on the road by Butterfly Forest are the same colours (`road_butterfly`), and in the train ending she hears the words.
- The tunnel is the edge. Inside it, Castle is **undecided**: the roads, the distances, which doors are locked, which warnings are true. It settles into one shape when someone arrives, and stays that shape for them. Julie's map "was right when I drew it". This is why every run's county is different, and the game never apologises for it.
- **By day Castle is the town that was. By night it is what it became.** The floating things sing about this because it is the only rule the county still keeps, and the bell is how everyone knows which one they are in.

## 2. What happened

1. The county's gold was never just gold. It was the thing that held Castle in place. The old families knew. They took a little, for the bench (potions: the Museum's MAGIC wing), and left the rest in the hill.
2. **Gnox Goldskin** bought the hill and dug all of it. He was not a wizard then. He had the gold cast into one sphere, **the Ball**, because he liked to look at it, and kept it in a hollow at the back of the mine's vault (**the seam**, which is still there, the size of a fist). With what came out of the hill he built the Works and the **Company School** on the hill, and the town sent its children up to it gladly (the portrait's second plate in the Museum). The more he took, the less the county could decide what it was. Nights got longer.
3. **Amos Noone**, the Company's timekeeper, rang the Works onto its shifts: the day shift at six, the night shift at nine (§8). When the nights got long he rang the whole county in. **The night of the bell**, one spring night, was the first: whoever was outside when the county changed its mind was put on his night shift and never relieved. That is what the undead are: **townsfolk who were outside when the county changed its mind**. The night shift walks Cinder Walk from nine to six.
4. That night **the Headmaster** took the children down the mine, because the bell said *in* and the mine was the Company's, and in is where he has kept them: his register, forty-one names, every one marked present, countersigned A. NOONE. Forty-one coats on the School's pegs, forty-one names on the memorial, the last HALE, R. The children are never shown and never enemies; what she fights in the mine are the dead miners, the clerk, the pit boss.
5. **Julie** is a witch, "a kind gentle witch" **[2020]**, from one of the old families. She held a **shield** over the town with what little gold was left, from the desk in the study under her house: inside it, days stayed days. It is why the Lowfields still look normal and the lamps still work. It cost her. Time moved strangely for her; a letter she wrote last month is a hundred years old. She sent the ones who were still well to Butterfly Forest, which was a garden then (Dr Vane gave her the list of everyone he had signed fit). She went ahead of the girl she was writing to and left what she would need where she would need it: a page in the Headmaster's confiscated drawer, one folded in the Shot-Firer's glove, the library's last page, an orb on the stand from her own study wired into the Factory's generator (the foreman's letter came back corrected in her hand), the gardener's page.
6. Goldskin died holding the Ball and did not stop. He had himself buried with it in the old burial ground, and his **four favourites** with him, one to a corner, all arranged on the same morning: **the keeper of his snakes** (the Snake), **his gardener** (the Flower), **his housekeeper**, who would not have a web swept in his house (the Spider), and **his soldier** (the Soldier, who still gets flowers). The Burial's notice names them in the order they were dug. **He is the wizard.** Julie scratched *leave it there* under DO NOT on the Hoar Stone, and locked his stair up to the bell from below with a key she kept on her ring: NOT FOR HIM.
7. The shield is failing. Julie wrote to the only family she has, because a witch's work passes down a family line and **Jane is one and does not know it** **[2020]**. Then she stopped being able to write.

## 3. Where Julie is

**[2020]** The earliest board says, in one card: *"Auntie is a doggy."* The 2020 build split them. **[decided]** The split is undone quietly. Holding the shield used up the person. What is left is the dog: it knows everything Julie knew, talks like someone who has explained this before, waits on her stoop, keeps her key. It never says it is her. It may not fully know. Jane is never told. The player can work it out: the dog is never seen at night; the letter's hand is the note's, the bowl's label's, the hatches' chalk, the Hoar Stone's scratches, the label on her Other Key; and each ending's last page puts a hand on a head.

**What is built:**

- The dog is **not there between 21:00 and 06:00** once the key is given. It never vanishes while she is looking: she walks away, comes back, and the step is empty. No line explains it. (Quests are not handed in at night.)
- "She is not dead." (the Burial's hand-in); the whole line, "She is not dead, {name}. I would know.", is kept for every ending. "She would have done it herself, once." "Do not ask how I know."
- Julie's bed: "made, recently and badly, by someone without the hands for it"; "When you open your eyes the blanket has been pulled up."
- The poke chain (`VOICE.md` rule 0): it counted the lamps going out, fourteen, "a different number if you count".
- The bowl: "DOG. Twice a day. It will tell you if you forget."
- The study desk: a clean ring in the dust the size of the stand the kitchen orb sits on; the Factory's orb stands on the same stand. The dog, taking the Factory back: "Somebody carried it all the way to the Works the winter before you came."

**Who sends her where.** The dog gives every step of the spine by day, and takes each back on the step, or for the far acts at a place it names nearer the work (the Museum's steps, the graveyard gate, the top of Church Lane: `WORLD.md` §3.6). Each hand-in names the next place and the key she already holds for it: the mine's vault, the Museum's brass key; the Museum's case, the green-tagged forest key; the forest's case marked NIGHT WATCHMAN, the Works; the Factory's locker, the Chairman's Key to the stair under the Hoar Stone; the stair behind Goldskin comes up in the School, and Julie's Other Key, which the dog gives with the School, opens it. Taking the School back it gives the last thing it asks: **Yours to Say** (§10). It never names Goldskin as the thing under the stone before she has seen him.

**Rules for writing it:** the dog never says "I", "me" or "my" about anything Julie did, and never says "she" about anything it could only know by having been her. **It slips twice in the whole game, both times under pressure and both times behind a question she chooses to ask:** in the forest ("Who sent them?" / "I did." / "She did. It was the kindest place she had, and it was a garden then.") and at the choice ("What would she do?" / "I held it." / "She held it. Nobody asked her, and she did not ask anybody."). The mine, the Museum, the Factory and the Burial have no slip; that is decided, so the two land. Nobody else in Castle has ever seen Julie and the dog together, and nobody remarks on it.

## 7. Choices for John

All five decided, 26 September 2026; §10 and §11 are what the fourth and the note under them became.

1. **The dog is what is left of Julie.** Yes. §3.
2. **Goldskin is the wizard.** Yes. One cause ties the mine, the Factory and the Burial.
3. **The undead are townsfolk**, the ones who were outside when the county changed its mind. Yes. The Headmaster and the mine land harder for it; killing them is uneasy, not heroic.
4. **The ending is a choice with no right answer.** Yes: hold the shield as Julie did, put the Ball back in the hill (the night goes, and everyone in it, the dog too), or take the Sunday train. Each has its own short epilogue, and each pays off the dog. Built: §10.
5. **Tone ceiling.** Unease, dread and sadness: yes. Gore, cruelty to children on screen, jump scares: no. The children are never shown as enemies.

**And the town notices.** Castle reacts as things happen: what she has done changes what people say, where they stand and what the county shows, from the next time she meets it, never in front of her by magic. Built: §11.

## 8. The bell at nine, and who rings it

**Amos Noone, Timekeeper, Goldskin Mining Co.** He rang the Works onto its shifts: the day shift at six, the night shift at nine. The School on the hill was the Company's school, and its bell was his. When the nights got long and Julie's shield began to fail, somebody had to tell the county when to be in, and he did: the bell at nine calls everybody in, and the bell at six lets them out. It is why the town goes indoors at the bell without knowing who started it, and why the night shift walks Cinder Walk from nine to six.

- **The register** in the top room is his time book: the cloth says REGISTER, the spine under it says G.M.Co. TIME BOOK, every page is signed A. NOONE, TIMEKEEPER, and nobody is absent. Further back, in the hand from the letter: JULIE, 9 o'c., the night of the bell, no time out. The last line is {name}: she is on the books now, and nothing earlier in the game writes her name in a register (the scarf's row has no letters yet; the stump's new cut is not yet lettered), so this is the first. The Headmaster's register in the mine is countersigned by the same hand.
- **The night of the bell**: Mr Tace went up the hill for Peter; Robert Hale went out without his cap; the Headmaster took the children down the mine. Against DUNN, E. the time book says *9 o'c., the night of the bell*, and no time out.
- **The pun is the point.** "Who rings the bell?" Noone. The vicar has "stopped answering it"; Mr Lyle says it is the church; the dog says only "Go and see who".
- **The bell is rung, not described** (built 27 September 2026): every stroke the county hears is one action, `ring { strikes, from, church }`, and one event, `Bell { strikes, at, church }`, which the sound of the game listens for. Nine at nine and six at six from the School on the hill (`data/clock.json`); nine at ten to nine on a Tuesday when the omen is true (`data/clock/omens.json`); one at each of the Timekeeper's phases, from the rope in his hands (`data/units/school.json`); and five from the church at six in the evening, for evensong, which is not the same bell (Miss Orme). Once `bell_stopped` is set the School's bell rings nothing, and the church goes on.
- **The fight** keeps its mechanics (`DUNGEONS.md` §3.6); the unit is The Timekeeper, the toasts are his bell, and the three that stand at the edge of the light are men in Company overalls. When he falls "the rope goes slack and stays slack", and **the bell stops** (§11): at nine, "Nine o'clock. No bell.", and the night comes all the same.

## 11a. What playing it through changed (27 September 2026)

Playing the spine end to end with the headless Reader (`VERIFICATION.md` §4.3) found places where the rows and this account disagreed, or the story left a gap. Each was settled in the story's favour:

- **The green-tagged key is kept.** The council key opens the ruined library and then the forest gate (§3's "each hand-in names the next place and the key she already holds"); it was spent on the library door and the forest could not be opened. It is bound now, as every key the dog sends her on with is.
- **The pipes come up inside the Works.** Not Relieved says she goes down the grate and "up into the Factory"; the outfall's manhole let her out in the night shift's yard instead. It leads to the Factory's own entry now (`data/doors.json`, `data/dungeons/pipes.json`).
- **The bell is rung** (§8): the action and event every stroke of the School's bell and the church's go through.
- **The bosses fight as `DUNGEONS.md` describes them.** Where a row had drifted from its design (the Attendant's, the Foreman's and the Timekeeper's health; the Shot-Firer's charge that burned without a tell; the Collector's net that felled rather than snared; the Emperor's buds, its feeding and its healing on the way home; the Burial's keepers borrowing spells at full strength; the School's heat) it was brought back, and `DUNGEONS.md` §3 says which and why under each dungeon.
- **What the dog keeps from Under the House.** The rat meat is hers to keep ("It wants you to keep them"), and the Stranglethorn it pays for is the Burial's bait: the Reader brews it at the bench before the Burial.
