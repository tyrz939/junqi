# Jane — Voice

How Castle talks. Every sign, note, item description, quest text, toast and line of dialogue is written against this file, and John's redlines become rules here. Pair with `STORY.md` (what is true) and `PLAN.md` §5 (how claims come true).

**Status: draft for review.**

## The model

> GOLDSKIN MINING Co. CLOSED. No entry. No exit either, some nights.

Why it works, so it can be done on purpose:

1. **It is an ordinary notice.** Company name, capitals, a rule. Someone official wrote it.
2. **It says one thing too many**, flatly, in the same register as the rest.
3. **The extra thing is checkable.** You can go in and find out. That is the difference between unease and decoration.
4. **It does not explain.** No "beware", no ellipsis, no exclamation mark.
5. **"Some nights."** It is uncertain about itself.

## Tone

A slight **Silent Hill** lean, on purpose. Not horror: a town with a second town underneath it, and a bell that tells you which one you are in.

What to take from it:

- **An ordinary place first.** A station, a supermarket, a school. The wrongness is administrative: notices, timetables, closing times.
- **Mist and short sight.** You see less than you want to. Light reaches further than it protects.
- **A signal that the world has changed.** Theirs was a siren; ours is the School bell at nine. After it, the same streets are a different place.
- **Nobody explains.** People talk around the thing. The dog most of all.
- **Guilt and grief under it, never gore over it.** The undead were neighbours. The Headmaster is still taking the register.
- **The map is not to be trusted**, and you still need it.

What to leave: jump scares, body horror, cruelty shown on screen, radio static as a gimmick, nonsense for its own sake. If a line is merely weird, cut it. It has to be *checkable* weird (rule 4).

## Rules

0. **A strange line needs a reason to be said.** "I am not a city dog" was a good line in a bad place: dropped into the first conversation it was only random. It now sits at the bottom of a chain you reach by bothering the dog five times, where it answers something ("you keep coming back to look; that is a city habit") and leads somewhere worse ("I stayed and counted the lamps going out. Fourteen. It is a different number if you count."). **Oddness is earned by context and escalates; it is never an opener.** Hiding the best lines behind persistence is encouraged: poke chains, the third reading of a sign, the note you only find at night.
1. **Proper English.** Correct grammar and spelling, plain words. The 2020 letter's broken English ("I have already draw a map") was an accident, not a style; it is gone. Unease comes from *what* is said, never from how badly.
2. **Plain, short, declarative.** One or two sentences. If a line needs a third, it needs cutting.
3. **One wrong detail per text.** Not two. The rest must be so ordinary that the wrong thing has something to stand out against.
4. **Make claims that can be tested.** "The lamps go out at ten." "Nobody has drowned in the lake." "It was not there yesterday." A line that cannot come true or fail to is atmosphere, and atmosphere is rationed: one in five at most.
5. **Never confirm.** No text says whether another text was true. The quest log records what Jane did, not what it meant.
6. **Uncertain about itself.** "Some nights." "Usually." "As far as anyone knows." "It was right when I drew it." The writer is honest and does not have all the facts.
7. **Practical people wrote these.** Shopkeepers, a headmaster, a company, a farmer, an aunt. They are warning you about closing times and wet paint. The horror is that the register never changes when the subject does.
8. **No fantasy diction.** No "beware", "thou", "ancient evil", "dark forces", "adventurer", "quest". Castle has a supermarket and a train timetable.
9. **No jokes that wink.** Dry is fine: the dog is dry. Nothing acknowledges it is a game. No pop culture.
10. **Jane notices; she does not emote.** Her observations are physical: smell, temperature, weight, what is missing. Never "I have a bad feeling".
11. **Julie is always practical and always one fact short.** Her notes tell you how to do the thing and not why.
12. **Names are plain and slightly off.** Gnox Goldskin is the limit, and he is 2020's. Places: the Lowfields, the Waters, the Works. People: the Headmaster, the clerk, the foreman. Titles, not fantasy names.
13. **Numbers and times are specific.** "At ten", "three crossings", "every ninth step". Specific is frightening; vague is cosy.
14. **Item descriptions carry one fact you can use and, sometimes, one you would rather not have.** "Opens any plain lock once, then it is the lock's."

## Registers

| Where | Sounds like | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Company / council notices | Stencilled, capitals, liability | "BRIDGE INSPECTED 14 MARCH. SAFE FOR FOOT TRAFFIC. ONE AT A TIME, PLEASE. IT PREFERS THAT." |
| Shop and farm signs | Hand-painted, polite, tired | "Eggs. Honesty box. Please don't look in the barn. Thank you." |
| Carved stone | Few words, older grammar is *not* needed | "DO NOT. Underneath, scratched later: it is not locked. that is not the problem." |
| Julie's handwriting | Lists, instructions, margins | "Dust, water, pansy. Make it before you go down any stairs. If the cellar is cold, come back up. If it is warm, come back up faster." |
| The dog | Dry, short, never reassuring | "You can sleep here. I will watch the door. I always have." |
| Townsfolk | Small talk with one hole in it | "Lovely evening. You'll want to be in before the lamps, mind. Not that anything happens. It's just what we do." |
| Jane (examining) | Physical, brief | "The kettle is warm. The dust on the table is not disturbed." |
| Quest text | What is asked, by whom, and what they did not say | "The farmer wants his three scarecrows counted at dusk and again at dawn. He would not say what number he is hoping for." |
| Toasts / system | Neutral, never cute | "The gate drops behind you." |

## Do not write

| | Why |
| --- | --- |
| "Beware, traveller! Evil lurks within..." | Fantasy diction, ellipsis, explains, cannot be tested |
| "A spooky old sign. It gives you the creeps." | Tells the feeling. Jane notices; she does not emote |
| "The sign says KEEP OUT. lol good luck with that" | Winks |
| "Something terrible happened here long ago." | Vague. What? When? Say the checkable part or say nothing |
| "I have already draw a map for you" | Broken English is not eerie. It is only broken |

## Writing an omen

An omen is a text plus a consequence (`PLAN.md` §5). Template:

```
where     a sign on the east road, first lamp post past the farm
text      "STREET LIGHTING: these lamps are switched off at 22:00 to save the county money.
           Residents are advised that this has always been the case."
claim     the lamps on this road go out at ten
if true   at 22:00 every lamp on this road segment goes dark; night spawns apply
if false  the lamps burn all night; the sign is just a sign
fair      the road is still walkable; the next lit hub is under two minutes away
tell      none. Optional: a townsperson who says "they never used to"
```

Checks before it ships: is it an ordinary notice first? Is there exactly one wrong thing? Can the player find out? Is being wrong about it survivable? Does it belong to this region?
